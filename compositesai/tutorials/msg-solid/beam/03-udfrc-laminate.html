03 UDFRC cross-ply laminate#

Step 1: A 3-D beam SG? CompositesAI offers a 2-D section#

Prompt

Build a 3-D OpenSG beam SG of a [90/0/90/0] laminate (listed from the bottom): 18 mm wide, four 1 mm layers, section centred at the origin, one 1 mm period long in x1. Each layer holds one fiber through its thickness, square pack, Vf = 40%: 0° fibers along x1, 90° fibers along x2. Fiber E = 276 GPa, ν = 0.28, matrix E = 4.76 GPa, ν = 0.37, in N, mm, MPa. Show the mesh.

CompositesAI

I can’t create a supported 3-D OpenSG beam SG for this case: OpenSG’s 3-D beam route is unverified, lacks a builder, and cannot correctly form the density/mass matrix for 3-D elements.

I can instead build either:

  1. a supported 2-D solid beam section of the \(18 \times 4\ \text{mm}\) laminate, or

  2. a periodic 3-D solid unit-cell SG (\(1 \times 18 \times 4\ \text{mm}\)) for effective 3-D properties.

Which would you like? If you need mass properties, please provide fiber and matrix densities; otherwise, I’ll set density to zero.

Step 2: The 2-D section with homogenized plies#

Prompt

use 2D cross section

CompositesAI

Built and checked the 2‑D Timoshenko solid-beam SG.

  • Section: 18 mm wide × 4 mm thick, centered at origin.

  • Layup, bottom → top: [90/0/90/0], each ply 1 mm.

  • Mesh: 576 quadratic quad9 elements (144 per ply).

  • UD ply properties were homogenized from the square-pack fiber/matrix cell at meshed \(V_f=0.39870\) (requested 40%).

  • Density set to zero, so any later beam mass matrix will be zero.

  • The 1 mm x1 periodic fiber geometry is represented by homogenized UD ply properties in this supported 2‑D section model.

Files: laminate_90_0_90_0_beam.yaml and laminate_90_0_90_0.msh. The mesh is shown above.

Step 3: Euler-Bernoulli and Timoshenko beam laws#

Prompt

Homogenize this section with OpenSG’s solid beam model. Give the Euler-Bernoulli 4×4 and the Timoshenko 6×6 stiffness and compliance, and the tension and shear centres.

CompositesAI

Homogenization completed using the 576-element quad9 section mesh. Both laws are symmetric and positive definite.

Rows/columns use:

  • Euler–Bernoulli: \([e_{11},\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3]\)

  • Timoshenko: \([e_{11},\gamma_{12},\gamma_{13},\kappa_1,\kappa_2,\kappa_3]\)

Values use the N–mm–MPa system; stiffness maps generalized strains to \([F_1,M_1,M_2,M_3]\) or \([F_1,F_2,F_3,M_1,M_2,M_3]\).
