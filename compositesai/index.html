CompositesAI#

CompositesAI is a chat assistant for the analysis of composite materials and structures. You ask in plain words. Its chat model asks for the inputs it needs, calls analysis tools, hands longer jobs to an agent that runs analysis skills, and explains the results with a search of its composites knowledge base. For beam cross-sections, plates and laminates, unit cells and meshes it runs OpenSG, unless you name another solver.

OpenSG inside CompositesAI#

OpenSG reaches CompositesAI in two ways.

Chat tools

Five functions that the chat model calls directly, for OpenSG's solid-element engine. laminate_sg_yaml writes the SG yaml of a laminate. sg_yaml_from_msh converts a gmsh mesh, and sg_yaml_from_sgio converts the SG file of another code (SwiftComp, VABS, Abaqus or gmsh). homogenize_sg runs the homogenization, and read_sg_out reads an OpenSG or VABS result file. The tools suit small SGs and return numbers only, no pictures.

Agent skills

Seven OpenSG skills run in an agent task, with files, larger meshes and pictures. The agent works on the files of the task in its own workspace, runs OpenSG there and sends its pictures back with the reply. Only the skills reach OpenSG's shell engine and dehomogenization.

Three builder skills write the SG yaml. Four model skills, one per OpenSG model, compute with it. Each model skill gives the homogenized properties and, when you give a load, the local stress and strain.

skill

what it does

opensg-section-builder

a beam cross-section from a PreVABS XML or a section definition (airfoil coordinates, a NACA 4-digit number or a circle, with layups, regions and webs): the 1-D shell ring by default, the 2-D solid section on request

opensg-cell-builder

a unit cell or a solid mesh: gmsh unit cells, a rectangle section, the meshes of other codes (gmsh, SwiftComp, VABS, Abaqus) and mesh levels for a convergence check

opensg-laminate-builder

a laminate or a sandwich layup: the 1-D layup SG and its layup picture

opensg-shell-beam

a thin-walled section as a ring of shell walls: the Timoshenko 6x6, the classical 4x4 and the mass matrix, a layup swap on the same ring, and every ply's in-plane stresses S11, S22 and S12 under the six beam loads. The transverse stresses S13, S23 and S33 and the stress peaks at web junctions are not recovered reliably.

opensg-solid-beam

a cross-section of solid elements: the Timoshenko 6x6, the Euler-Bernoulli 4x4, the mass matrix and the section centres, and the local stresses under the axial force, the torque and the bending moments (F1, M1, M2, M3). The shear forces F2 and F3 are not recovered.

opensg-solid-plate

a laminate, a periodic panel cell or a 3-D core cell: the ABD 6x6 or the Reissner-Mindlin 8x8, and the ply and local stresses under plate forces and moments

opensg-solid-3d

a microstructure: the effective 3-D stiffness 6x6, its compliance and the nine engineering constants, and the constituent stresses under a macro stress or strain

A builder hands its yaml to the model skill of its model. The section builder hands over to the shell beam or the solid beam, the laminate builder to the solid plate, and the cell builder to the solid 3-D, the solid plate or the solid beam. Woven cells are the exception: CompositesAI's textile skill meshes them and SwiftComp computes them, unless you ask for OpenSG. SwiftComp and VABS also run when you name them, or for what OpenSG does not compute, such as a thermal conductivity or a failure index.

The skills never invent a material constant, a dimension, a density, an angle or a load. When an input is missing, they ask for it or go on with stated defaults. A named common material, such as aluminium 6061 or A36 steel, is filled in with its usual values. Every assumed value is listed in the reply.

How to read a tutorial#

Each step is a prompt, then CompositesAI's response.

To try one, open a chat at compositesai.com and send each prompt in turn. Tutorials 02, 04, 06 and 08 continue the chats of 01, 03, 05 and 07, so send their prompts in the same chat.
