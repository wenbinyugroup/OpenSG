==================================================================================================================================================
WHAT THE tet10 PAIR CAN AND CANNOT SUPPORT
==================================================================================================================================================

Each density has exactly TWO tet10 points, so the observed order of convergence is NOT
measurable and no genuine (3-point) Richardson extrapolation is possible.  The d% columns
above are the DIFFERENCE between the two tet10 meshes, not the error of the finer one.
Assuming an order p, that error is  diff / (r_h^p - 1)  with r_h = (N_fine/N_coarse)^(1/3):

Read the p=1 column as the pessimistic bound and p=2 as the expected one.
At rho = 0.3 every term is <= 0.042% even at p = 1 -- the verdict is safe under any
assumed order.  At rho = 0.05 the G error of the finest tet10 is ~0.31% at p = 2 and
~0.69% at p = 1, i.e. 2 to 4 times the 0.174% two-point difference; quote 0.3-0.7%, not
0.17%, if G at rho = 0.05 is used quantitatively.

The tet4 legs DO have enough points for an observed order (4 at rho = 0.05, 3 at rho = 0.3).
Measured over the finest interval, deviation from the converged tet10 law falls at:
   rho = 0.05   A11 p=1.57   A66 p=1.53   D11 p=1.74   D66 p=1.61   G11 p=2.03   G22 p=2.03
   rho = 0.3    A11 p=2.04   A66 p=2.05   D11 p=2.06   D66 p=2.02   G11 p=2.12   G22 p=2.11

So G is NOT slower in RATE than A11/D11 -- at both densities its tet4 order is ~2, the
same as the others.  G is simply far larger in MAGNITUDE at rho = 0.05 (77% vs 15% on the
coarsest mesh), which is what makes it the last term to become usable.
