05 Beam cross-section homogenization#

The cell of the plate tutorials can also be read as the cross-section of a beam. This tutorial computes the Timoshenko law of that beam.

The SG#

examples/OpenSG-solid/7_get_beam_props_from_SG/RHC_SW_2UC_45.yaml holds the mesh of the plate tutorial: 4251 nodes and 6640 triangles, in mm. The run draws the same mesh picture. The header asks for a shear-refined beam. The materials are in MPa:

n_model: 1      # 1 = beam, 2 = plate, 3 = solid -- the macro model this SG homogenizes to
refined: 1      # 0 = classical (plate ABD / beam EB); 1 = shear-refined (plate ABDG / beam Timoshenko)
msg: solid      # the ENGINE this SG belongs to (opensg_solid); `opensg <yaml>` dispatches on it
...
materials:
  1:
    type: 2
    aux: [0.0, 0.0]
    C:
    - [35696.6, 27696.6, 3251.1, 0.0, 0.0, -25368.7]
    - [27696.6, 35696.6, 3251.1, 0.0, 0.0, -25368.7]
    - [3251.1, 3251.1, 8917.8, 0.0, 0.0, -487.1]
    - [0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 3500.0, -500.0, 0.0]
    - [0.0, 0.0, 0.0, -500.0, 3500.0, 0.0]
    - [-25368.7, -25368.7, -487.1, 0.0, 0.0, 27958.5]
  2:
    type: 2
    aux: [0.0, 0.0]
    C:
    - [35696.6, 27696.6, 3251.1, 0.0, 0.0, 25368.7]

    - [0.0, 0.0, 0.0, 500.0, 3500.0, 0.0]

  3:
    type: 0
    aux: [0.0, 0.0]
    E: 69000.0
    nu: 0.3

Materials 1 and 2 are the two face plies, stored as rotated 6x6 stiffness matrices (type: 2). They differ in the sign of their coupling terms. Material 3 is isotropic with E = 69000 MPa and nu = 0.3.

Axis 1, the beam axis, is normal to the mesh picture. Axis 2 runs along y2, the horizontal axis of the picture, and axis 3 along y3, its vertical axis. OpenSG labels them y1 and y2, after the first and the second node coordinate.

Run it#

Run it in the example folder. From the root of the repository:

cd examples/OpenSG-solid/7_get_beam_props_from_SG
opensg RHC_SW_2UC_45.yaml

  • omega 1: the law is the stiffness of the whole section, per unit length of the beam.

  • The rows and the columns are [eps11 gam12 gam13 kappa1 kappa2 kappa3], the order the run prints: extension, the two transverse shears, twist and the two bending curvatures.

  • The compliance is the inverse of the stiffness.

With N and mm, the block of rows and columns 1-3 is in N, the block 4-6 in N mm^2 and the coupling blocks in N mm. The diagonal:

What the numbers say:

  • EI2 is more than twice EI3. Bending about axis 2 strains the face plies, which sit about 23 mm from that axis.

  • The largest coupling is C45 = -3.1861510E+006 N mm^2, between twist and bending about axis 2.

  • The .out has no mass matrix because no material block has a density. A density: key in the material blocks adds The 6X6 Mass Matrix to the file.

Set refined: 0 in the header to get the classical 4x4 law instead. The .out then holds The Effective Euler-Bernoulli Beam Stiffness Matrix, for extension, twist and the two bending curvatures.
